# AdSense, from Google’s own pages

Checked 2026-11-14. This is what Google publishes. It is not a guess about how a reviewer behaves.

## Eligibility

`https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724`

- You need your own content that meets the policies, and you must be at least 18.
- The content must be high-quality, original, and attract an audience.
- You must be able to edit the HTML of the site you submit.
- The site must comply with the program policies. Google says the policies can change and keeping up is the publisher’s job.
- There is no page-count minimum, no age minimum, and no traffic minimum on this page.

## Pages ready for review

`https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563`

- Unique content that is relevant to visitors.
- Clear navigation.
- Original text, not only embedded videos or other people’s articles.
- Google ads may not be placed on scraped or copyrighted content. Google points that rule at the publisher policies.

## Why accounts are not approved

`https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/81904`

Google says it may review all pages, not only the URL where the code was placed. The content reasons it lists:

- Insufficient content: too little text, or the site looks under construction. Pages need complete sentences and paragraphs, not only headlines. Do not apply while the site is a template. An empty test page with only the ad code is not approved.
- Content quality: not enough original content that gives a user a reason to visit. Do not put the code on auto-generated pages or pages with little original content. Affiliate content should be a minor part of the site if it adds no other value. Google points publishers at the Search spam policies.
- Policy violations, broken navigation, under-construction pages, paid-to-click traffic, and an unsupported language.

## What Google’s policy-education video says about size

In the AdSense Site Approvals video on quality content (`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=poU80MgSvrY`), the Google speaker says a better site with fewer pages will be more effective than a large website with poor-quality content. The same video says the site needs enough content for Google to tell what it is about, that duplicate copy and duplicate articles are a problem, and that the publisher’s own knowledge is what makes the page original.

That sentence is the direct answer to “a bigger site gets approved faster.” Google does not say that. Google says the opposite trade: quality over page count.

## Required legal pages

`https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1348695` and `https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7549925`

- A privacy policy must tell visitors that third-party vendors, including Google, use cookies to serve ads based on prior visits.
- It must point at Google’s Ads Settings, or at `www.aboutads.info`, for opting out of personalized advertising.
- If other ad networks are used, name them and link their opt-outs.
- Cookie disclosure is required even before ads render if the ad tag is on the page.
- Google will not write the policy for you.

EEA, UK, and Switzerland visitors also need a consent mechanism under Google’s EU user consent policy (`https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/16758589`). A US-focused tractor site still gets those visitors. Plan the consent banner before the first ad tag, not after a rejection.

## What actually helps an application

From those pages, the things that match the written rules are:

- The site is fully built. Navigation works. There are no “coming soon” holes in the menu.
- Pages have original sentences that a compact-tractor owner cannot get from a copied spec table.
- The site is not mostly affiliate links.
- Specs are cited, not scraped from another publisher.
- A privacy policy and a contact page exist.
- The ad code is on a real page, and the rest of the site would still make sense if the ads were removed.

A large count of thin model pages is one of the patterns the rejection article describes as auto-generated or low-original-content. Shipping them to “get to approval faster” raises the chance that Google reviews those pages and refuses the account. After a refusal, the fix is to change the site and ask again. That is slower than applying once with pages that already meet the written bar.
