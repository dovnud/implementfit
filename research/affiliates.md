## Verified from the program’s own pages

### Amazon Associates

- **Network:** Amazon Associates, direct. Not Impact or CJ.
- **Standard commission:** 3.00% of qualifying revenue for Lawn & Garden, Tools, Outdoors, Home, Home Improvement, and the other categories listed in the same cell of Table 1. Also on that table: Toys, Furniture, Pets, Headphones, Beauty, Musical Instruments, Business & Industrial Supplies, Sports, Baby Products, and Amazon Coins, all at the same 3.00%.
- **Source:** Associates Program Standard Commission Income Statement, Table 1, fetched 2026-11-14 from `https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ`. The page does not show an “as of” date. Rates change. Re-read the table before launch.
- **Cookie / session:** The Operating Agreement, fetched via its public text in search and consistent with the help pages, defines a session that ends at the first of: 24 hours after the click, the customer placing an order (with a digital-product exception), or the customer using a different associate’s Special Link. An item added in that session can still qualify if the order is completed no later than 89 days after the click. Source: `https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies/`. A separate help page says the cart “usually” expires after 90 days (`https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G9SMD8TQHFJ7728F`). Use 24 hours and 89 days from the Operating Agreement, and treat “90 days” as the help desk’s rounding.
- **What this pays for on this site:** filters, fluids, hand tools, some implements. It pays poorly for a $2,000 cutter that the reader buys next week after the 24-hour session is gone, unless the item was already in the cart.
- **Other Amazon tables:** Onsite and Creator Ads schedules are different documents and different rates. Do not mix them into the website commission. One of them is `https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G4ARBJC7Z2NK48CA`.

## Disclosure and policy

- The FTC Endorsement Guides require a clear disclosure when a link can pay the publisher. Reference: `https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking`. Put it on the page, not only in a footer policy, and put it above the first paid link.
- Say that a retailer link may be paid. Say that specs are not from the retailer.
- Amazon’s agreement requires its own disclosure language and forbids some uses of Associates links. Read the current Operating Agreement, not a blog summary, before the first link.
- Do not bid on manufacturer trademarks in paid search unless that program allows it. Several implement programs prohibit it. Confirm in the contract.
- Track the contracted rate, cookie, and the date the contract was read in a private note. The public page is not the contract. Titan’s page is the example: it currently asserts two commissions.
